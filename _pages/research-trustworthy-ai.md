## Research Overview

Artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into critical computing systems, cybersecurity operations, autonomous platforms, and organizational decision-making. While AI introduces powerful new capabilities, it also creates vulnerabilities and failure mechanisms that challenge traditional approaches to security, reliability, and system assurance.

At WASP Lab, we investigate how AI systems fail, how adversaries exploit their weaknesses, and how these systems can be engineered to operate securely and dependably under uncertainty.

Our research examines both **the security of AI systems themselves** and **the reliability of systems that depend on AI**.

---

## Established Research Foundations

Our research builds on three complementary areas.

### Adversarial Machine Learning & AI Security

We investigate security vulnerabilities and adversarial threats affecting machine learning systems.

Our work includes adversarial attacks, security assessment of machine learning models, and empirical evaluation of how adversarial behavior affects model performance and reliability.

We seek to understand the conditions under which AI models become vulnerable and develop evidence-based approaches to evaluating their security and robustness.

### Privacy-Preserving Machine Learning

We investigate approaches to protecting sensitive information during the development, training, and deployment of intelligent systems.

Our research includes privacy-preserving learning, federated learning, and the application of deep learning techniques in privacy-sensitive environments.

We examine the trade-offs among privacy, model utility, computational efficiency, and system security.

### Empirical Evaluation of Trustworthy AI

We investigate methods for systematically assessing AI performance, robustness, security, and operational limitations.

Our work emphasizes reproducible experiments, comparative evaluation, and the identification of conditions under which AI techniques succeed or fail.

This empirical foundation supports the development of more dependable intelligent systems.

Large language models and autonomous AI agents introduce new capabilities for reasoning, planning, content generation, and tool use.

However, these capabilities create new attack surfaces and failure mechanisms that extend beyond conventional machine learning security.

### Research Approach

Our research explores complementary approaches to improving AI security and dependability:

- **Adversarial evaluation:** Systematically investigating how malicious inputs, manipulated context, and adversarial interactions affect AI behavior.
- **Failure characterization:** Developing taxonomies and empirical methods for understanding failures in LLMs and autonomous agents.
- **Knowledge-based and neuro-symbolic approaches:** Integrating structured knowledge, constraints, and reasoning mechanisms to improve dependable AI behavior.
- **AI guardrails and runtime assurance:** Investigating detection, verification, and intervention mechanisms for unsafe or unreliable outputs and actions.
- **Resilience testing:** Evaluating AI systems under controlled disturbances, changing operational conditions, and unexpected component behavior.
- **Reproducible experimentation:** Developing datasets, benchmarks, and experimental frameworks to support rigorous evaluation.

Our long-term objective is to advance the engineering of **secure, trustworthy, and resilient AI systems**, particularly where AI capabilities are integrated into larger operational workflows.

## Research Contributions & Outcomes

Our research aims to contribute:

- New knowledge about vulnerabilities and failure mechanisms in AI systems.
- Empirical methods for evaluating adversarial robustness and AI security.
- Privacy-preserving and dependable machine learning techniques.
- Failure taxonomies and benchmarks for generative and agentic AI.
- Knowledge-based guardrails and runtime assurance mechanisms.
- Reproducible datasets, experimental prototypes, and open-source tools.
