RESEARCH THEME 01

Intelligent Cybersecurity
& Security Operations

Advancing intelligent threat detection, security analytics, cyber threat intelligence, and AI-driven security operations while engineering reliability, dependability, and resilience into cybersecurity systems.

Research Overview

Modern cybersecurity operations face increasing complexity, expanding attack surfaces, heterogeneous security data, and adaptive adversaries. These challenges create opportunities for artificial intelligence and automation, but also introduce new sources of uncertainty, operational risk, and system failure.

At WASP Lab, we investigate intelligent approaches to cybersecurity while examining the conditions under which these technologies succeed, fail, or produce unintended consequences.

Our objective is not simply to make cybersecurity operations more autonomous, but to make them more effective, reliable, dependable, and resilient.

Established Research Foundations

Our research in intelligent cybersecurity builds on three complementary areas.

Intelligent Threat Detection & Security Analytics

We investigate machine learning, data analytics, and intelligent approaches to detecting malicious activities, identifying suspicious behavior, and improving security monitoring.

Our research includes malware detection, behavioral analytics, anomaly detection, and empirical evaluation of machine learning techniques for cybersecurity.

A recurring objective is understanding how detection systems perform under realistic operational conditions, including changing data distributions, adversarial behavior, and limitations in training data.

Cyber Threat Intelligence & Knowledge Engineering

We investigate methods for extracting, integrating, representing, and reasoning over cybersecurity threat intelligence.

Our research explores natural language processing, information extraction, knowledge graphs, ontologies, and semantic approaches to improve the quality, interoperability, and operational usefulness of threat intelligence.

This work provides a foundation for security systems that can reason about threats using structured, contextualized security knowledge.

Security Automation & Adaptive Defense

We investigate intelligent approaches to security automation, adaptive defense, and automated security analysis.

Our work explores how security mechanisms can respond to evolving threats and changing operational conditions while maintaining effectiveness and limiting unintended consequences.

These research foundations inform our emerging work on increasingly autonomous cybersecurity systems.

Emerging Research: Reliable Agentic Security Operations

Artificial intelligence is changing how cybersecurity operations are conducted. Large language models and autonomous agents can increasingly support security investigation, threat intelligence analysis, incident triage, and response workflows.

However, greater autonomy does not automatically produce more dependable security operations.

Agentic systems introduce additional failure mechanisms arising from uncertain reasoning, complex workflows, heterogeneous information sources, and interactions with external tools and operational environments.

Our emerging research program investigates three interconnected classes of failure.

01

Cognitive Failures

Failures in interpreting evidence, reasoning about security events, planning, or making decisions under incomplete or misleading context.

02

Orchestration Failures

Failures in task decomposition, coordination, delegation, dependency management, and the execution of multi-stage security workflows.

03

Execution Failures

Failures arising from incorrect, incomplete, unsafe, or unintended actions during tool use and operational execution.

Research Approach

We investigate these failures through systematic characterization, measurement, mitigation, and experimental validation.

Our research explores several complementary approaches:

  • Task-driven security context enrichment: Providing intelligent systems with relevant operational and security knowledge required for dependable decisions.
  • Neuro-symbolic AI and knowledge engineering: Combining learned capabilities with structured knowledge, ontologies, and procedural reasoning.
  • Knowledge-based guardrails: Introducing constraints, verification mechanisms, and operational checks to reduce unsafe or incorrect behavior.
  • Chaos engineering: Introducing controlled disturbances and failures to evaluate robustness, recovery, and resilience across multi-stage security workflows.
  • Empirical evaluation: Developing reproducible testbeds, datasets, benchmarks, and failure-oriented evaluation methods.

Our long-term objective is to establish engineering methods for reliable, dependable, and resilient agentic cybersecurity systems.

Research Methodology

This research theme follows WASP Lab’s broader RDR Computing methodology:

01

Characterize

Identify failure modes, contributing conditions, and propagation mechanisms.

02

Quantify

Measure failure frequency, severity, impact, and operational consequences.

03

Mitigate

Develop mechanisms for prevention, detection, containment, and recovery.

04

Validate

Evaluate proposed solutions under realistic uncertainty and adversarial conditions.

Research Contributions & Outcomes

Our work seeks to advance both the scientific understanding and practical engineering of intelligent cybersecurity systems.

Research outcomes include:

  • Intelligent detection and cybersecurity analytics techniques.
  • Methods and resources for cyber threat intelligence extraction and integration.
  • Knowledge-based approaches to security reasoning and automation.
  • Failure taxonomies, benchmarks, and evaluation frameworks.
  • Methods for improving the dependability of autonomous security workflows.
  • Research datasets, experimental prototypes, and reproducible tools.
